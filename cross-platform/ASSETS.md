# Bundled recordings and licenses

React Native 0.2.0 initially reused these recordings without changing the WAV data. Version 0.2.7 extends only the shared app?s Chakri recording using the crossfaded edit documented below. Its visible objects are 94 original OBJ mesh parts generated by `tools/build_models.py`, with original procedural paper, normal, char, smoke, spark and contact-shading textures. No downloaded 3D models or branded packaging are used. The original-artwork section near the end describes the earlier Kotlin app's artwork; those illustrations are not used as placed objects in this version.

Updated 10 November 2026. Five source recordings were downloaded through Pixabay's public browser controls and mastered offline into six interactive effects. The app no longer depends on reaching Freesound.

## Mastering and provenance

Every output is 48 kHz mono PCM16. Gain is adjusted conservatively; pitch is unchanged. Clip edges use 10 ms fades and each track ends in 250 ms silence. A one-second excerpt of real sparkler hiss (oneSparkler, source seconds 16–17) supplies a virtual ignition cue; this is not claimed as an actual fuse recording.

Anar uses the fountain recording. Phuljhadi uses source seconds 34–48.1 of oneSparkler. Chakri uses the complete spinning-bee recording. Sky shot uses the complete explosion-with-echo recording, with ascent aligned to the lead-in. Sutli isolates source seconds 10.9–11.6 of CRACKER SOUND; its specific subtype is unverified. Ladi is an authored sequence of 36 pops extracted from the Diwali field recording; exact excerpt offsets and output timings are recorded in the manifest. It is not an untouched ladi field recording.

`app/src/main/assets/audio-manifest.json` is the precise machine-readable ledger: source URLs, file names, source/output SHA-256 hashes, trim boundaries, gain, cue times, ladi assembly edits and match limitations. The preparation script performs no network requests.

Source descriptions and waveforms were inspected; a physical listening/fidelity review and Poco M5 audiovisual timing review remain required. ## 0.2.10 Sutli mastering and runtime dynamics

The shared app's `sutli.wav` is regenerated from the original Kotlin mastered edit. Its one-second quiet ignition is retained byte-for-byte. The bang/decay adds a 0.6 mix of the source recording's 70-400 Hz band, uses a tanh soft transient drive of 4 and is normalized to a 0.96 full-scale peak. There is no synthesized sound, pitch change or hard digital clipping. SHA256: `6df625dfc81ffc45cc6bcd4f0601bc67d7ab613f463416e6f2d8bc6c27f56996`. Attribution and the generic Diwali-bang limitation remain unchanged. `assets/audio-manifest.json` records mastering parameters and immutable input/output hashes.

Anar, Chakri and Phuljhadi use runtime gain envelopes alongside particle power; their WAV data is unchanged by this update. Sutli plays at gain 1 before distance attenuation, ground effects at up to 0.65. Concurrent sky taps use four preloaded players, each at 0.45 / sqrt(active voices) to retain mixer headroom even for coinciding peaks. Mute applies to all players. `tools/build_effects.py` regenerates the Chakri loop and Sutli master from sibling original Kotlin WAV assets; it never processes its previous output again.

Visual behavior research for 0.2.10 is documented in [EFFECT_REFERENCES.md](EFFECT_REFERENCES.md). Supplier footage and sampled video storyboards are observation references only, not redistribution assets.

In 0.2.11, direct sky taps start playback at source time 3.25825 s (1.2 s before the 4.45825 s burst). Ground Sky shot retains its original full ignition/launch timeline. This is a playback offset, not a rewritten WAV or pitch change.
